# Post-Mortem: Read Replica Replication Lag Causes Missing Orders

## Service Overview

- **Incident date:** 2026-03-18, 01:40-02:55 UTC (75 minutes)
- **Affected service:** OrderService, which sends writes to the `OrdersDB` primary and order-history reads to the `OrdersDBReplica` streaming replica.
- **Impact:** Customers who placed an order were told it did not exist when they opened the confirmation page. About 2,300 `GET /orders/{id}` requests returned 404, and support received 140 tickets about missing orders.

## Common Error Signatures

### Replica Lag Warnings and 404 on Fresh Orders

```text
WARN  OrderService.read_router: replica lag 94.2s exceeds threshold 5s, routing anyway
INFO  GET /orders/7f3a9c21 -> 404 Not Found (order not found)
```

- **Observed:** Replication lag on `OrdersDBReplica` grew from under 1 second to a peak of 11 minutes.
- **Observed:** Orders were written successfully on the primary; only reads from the replica were missing them.

## Root Cause Analysis

### WAL Burst from the Nightly Archive Job

The nightly archive job moved 4 million completed orders to an archive table using a single large `UPDATE` and `DELETE`. This generated about 40 GB of WAL in under 20 minutes. The replica runs on a disk with lower IOPS than the primary and could not replay WAL fast enough, so replay fell further behind for over an hour.

How it was confirmed, on the replica:

```sql
SELECT now() - pg_last_xact_replay_timestamp() AS replay_lag;
```

### Read Router Ignored Lag

OrderService's read router logged a warning when lag exceeded 5 seconds but still sent reads to the replica. There was no fallback to the primary for reads that must see the caller's own recent writes.

## Playbook Remediation

### Immediate Mitigation

1. The archive job was paused. Lag recovered within 35 minutes.
2. Order-history reads were temporarily routed to the primary through a feature flag.
3. Progress was tracked on the primary:

   ```sql
   SELECT client_addr, state, write_lag, flush_lag, replay_lag
   FROM pg_stat_replication;
   ```

### Follow-up Actions

1. Rewrite the archive job to process rows in batches of 10,000 with a pause between batches, keeping WAL generation steady.
2. Make the read router fall back to the primary when lag exceeds 5 seconds, and always read a just-created order from the primary.
3. Move the replica to storage matching the primary's IOPS.
4. Add an alert when `replay_lag` stays above 30 seconds for 5 minutes.
